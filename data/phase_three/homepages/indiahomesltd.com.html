Letter from the Managing Director

A defining year of transformation.

FY26 marks a defining year for India Homes. We have completed the Company's strategic pivot from stainless‑steel manufacturing to Mumbai real‑estate redevelopment — signing a landmark joint venture with the Lloyds Group at Khopoli, fully repaying our borrowings with Kotak Mahindra Bank, and reaching a One‑Time Settlement with J.C. Flowers ARC to resolve our remaining secured debt.

With our debt position substantially resolved, a focused operating model and a visible launch pipeline across Wadala, Matunga, Chembur and Khopoli, the Company is now positioned to scale a high‑margin, asset‑light redevelopment business — and deliver meaningful value to shareholders in the years ahead.

Mr. Varun S. Gupta · Managing Director

— Process
Three routes, one rigorous method

Step by step. Plain language.

Co‑operative Housing Society

Society Redevelopment

For registered Co‑operative Housing Societies in Mumbai, governed by the 79(A) directives of the Maharashtra Co‑operative Societies Act and the 2019 Government Resolution that sets out the model procedure.

Governed by
79A GR, 4 Jul 2019
Consent threshold
≥ 51% to initiate · ≥ 75% to confirm
Typical timeline
36–48 months from DA
  1. 01
    Special General Body Meeting · initiating resolution

    Society convenes an SGBM with prescribed notice. A resolution to explore redevelopment is passed with at least 51% of total members of the society in attendance and voting in favour.

  2. 02
    Appoint a Project Management Consultant (PMC)

    An empanelled, independent PMC (typically an architect) is appointed to prepare the feasibility report, draft the tender and supervise the developer‑selection process.

  3. 03
    Tender · transparent developer selection

    The PMC issues a tender on terms approved by the General Body. Sealed bids are opened in the presence of members; commercials, carpet area entitlement and corpus are compared on a like‑for‑like basis.

  4. 04
    Confirming Special General Body Meeting · LOI

    The shortlisted developer (India Homes) is confirmed by at least 75% of the General Body. A Letter of Intent and Term Sheet are signed; the District Deputy Registrar is intimated.

  5. 05
    Development Agreement & Power of Attorney

    A registered Development Agreement is executed between the Society and India Homes, along with an Irrevocable Power of Attorney for execution of approvals only — not for sale of members' entitlements.

  6. 06
    Individual Permanent Alternate Accommodation Agreement (PAA)

    Each member signs an individual PAA with India Homes, recording new‑flat carpet area, rent, brokerage, corpus, shifting allowance and the schedule of handover.

  7. 07
    Approvals · IOD & Commencement Certificate

    Architect files plans with MCGM; Intimation of Disapproval (IOD) and Commencement Certificate (CC) are obtained. RERA registration is filed once plinth CC is in hand.

  8. 08
    Vacating possession · rent commences

    Members vacate against handover of cheques for rent, brokerage and corpus. Monthly rent runs by standing instruction until possession of the new flat.

  9. 09
    Demolition & construction

    Demolition, excavation, structure and finishing. Monthly progress is shared with the Society and the PMC. Quality audits are conducted at key construction milestones.

  10. 10
    Occupation Certificate & possession

    On receipt of the Occupation Certificate (OC), members are handed over their new flats along with the agreed corpus. Society is reformed with the new building's members.

Cessed Buildings · Island City

DCPR 33(7)

For cessed (pre‑1969) buildings in the Island City of Mumbai, classified by MHADA Repair Board as Category A, B or C. Provides higher FSI and incentive FSI for tenant rehabilitation.

Governed by
DCPR 2034, Reg. 33(7)
Consent threshold
≥ 51% of eligible tenants
Approving authority
MHADA · MCGM
  1. 01
    Building category & cess verification

    MHADA Repair Board categorisation (A / B / C), cess records and property card are pulled and verified. Structural audit assesses suitability for redevelopment.

  2. 02
    Tenant census & eligibility

    An eligibility list is prepared from cess receipts, ration cards and possession evidence. Each tenant's existing carpet area is measured and recorded.

  3. 03
    Developer appointment · tenants' resolution

    A meeting of all eligible tenants resolves to appoint India Homes as developer with at least 51% consent. Letter of Intent and Term Sheet are signed.

  4. 04
    MHADA NOC & Annexures

    Application is filed with MHADA Repair Board for the Section 79A1 NOC, along with eligibility annexures and architect's feasibility report.

  5. 05
    PAA with each tenant · registered

    A registered Permanent Alternate Accommodation Agreement is executed with each eligible tenant, recording new‑tenement carpet area, rent, corpus and shifting terms.

  6. 06
    IOD · Commencement Certificate

    Plans are approved by MCGM Building Proposal department; IOD and CC are obtained. RERA registration follows plinth CC.

  7. 07
    Tenants vacate · rent commences

    Tenants vacate the old building against handover of rent, brokerage, corpus cheques and shifting allowance. Rent runs monthly until possession of the new tenement.

  8. 08
    Demolition, construction & quality audit

    Construction proceeds with milestone‑based quality audits. Rehab tenements are built and finished to the specifications recorded in the PAA.

  9. 09
    OC & permanent possession

    Occupation Certificate is obtained; possession of new tenements is handed over to tenants along with corpus. Cess is discontinued; a co‑operative housing society of new owners is formed.

Slum Rehabilitation · SRA

DCPR 33(10)

For notified slums on private, government or municipal land under the Slum Rehabilitation Authority (SRA). Eligible slum dwellers receive free rehab tenements; developer is granted incentive FSI for free‑sale.

Governed by
DCPR 2034, Reg. 33(10)
Consent threshold
≥ 51% of eligible slum dwellers
Approving authority
Slum Rehabilitation Authority
  1. 01
    Eligibility survey · Annexure II

    SRA Competent Authority conducts an eligibility survey of slum dwellers based on the cut‑off date and documentary proof. The verified eligibility list is published as Annexure II.

  2. 02
    Society formation among slum dwellers

    Eligible dwellers form a proposed co‑operative housing society and obtain registration under the Maharashtra Co‑operative Societies Act.

  3. 03
    Developer appointment · ≥ 51% consent

    A General Body meeting appoints India Homes as developer with at least 51% consent of eligible members. Individual consent forms are collected with photographs and identification.

  4. 04
    Development Agreement & PAA with each member

    A registered Development Agreement is executed with the Society, and individual PAAs are executed with each eligible member recording the 27.88 sq m (300 sq ft) carpet area entitlement and free rehab terms.

  5. 05
    SRA Letter of Intent

    SRA issues the Letter of Intent (LOI) on satisfaction of eligibility and project viability, prescribing rehab and free‑sale components, FSI and conditions of approval.

  6. 06
    Intimation of Approval & transit camp

    SRA issues IOA. India Homes constructs or arranges transit accommodation. Eligible slum dwellers shift to transit and receive monthly rent until possession.

  7. 07
    Construction · rehab + free sale

    Rehab and free‑sale buildings are constructed. Rehab buildings are typically completed and handed over before free‑sale buildings, in line with SRA conditions.

  8. 08
    Occupation Certificate & allotment

    On OC, permanent allotment letters are issued by SRA. Members take possession of their rehab tenements; the new co‑operative housing society is constituted.
