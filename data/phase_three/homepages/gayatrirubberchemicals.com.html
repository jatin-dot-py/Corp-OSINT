What is flame retardant rubber?

Flame retardant rubber is an elastomer compounded with additives that slow ignition, self-extinguish once the flame source is removed, and limit smoke and toxic gas release. Unlike standard rubber, a fire resistant rubber grade is validated for flame spread, smoke density and toxicity before it can be used in enclosed spaces such as rail coaches, tunnels and electrical enclosures.

What is EN 45545-2 HL3?

EN 45545-2 is the European fire-safety standard for materials used in railway rolling stock. It defines three hazard levels, and HL3 is the most demanding — applied to sleeper cars, underground and high-speed services. HL3 rubber must pass heat release, smoke opacity and toxicity testing at the strictest limits of the standard.

Which industries use flame retardant rubber?

Railways and metro rail are the largest users, followed by defence vehicles, tunnelling and infrastructure, electrical and switchgear enclosures, solar and construction glazing, and general manufacturing. Typical parts include rubber profiles, gaskets, extrusions, grommets, bellows and moulded industrial rubber components.
