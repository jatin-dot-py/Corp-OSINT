How much should my newborn sleep?

Newborns typically sleep for about 16-20 hours a day, but their sleep is divided into short periods and may not follow a regular schedule.

view

When do babies start rolling over?

Most babies begin rolling over at around 4-6 months of age, although the timing can vary.

view

What are the typical feeding patterns for a newborn?

Newborns typically feed every 2-3 hours, as they have small stomachs. Breast milk upto 6 months is highly recommended or formula suggested by the paediatrician.

view

When do babies usually start sitting up? 

Babies can typically sit with support at around 4-7 months and sit independently at around 6-9 months.

view

What's the average age for a baby's first tooth to come in? 

Babies usually get their first tooth around 6 months, but the timing can vary greatly. 

view

How can I encourage my baby to start crawling? 

Place toys just out of reach to motivate your baby, provide tummy time, and create a safe space for exploration. 

view

What are the developmental milestones for a one-year-old? 

One-year-olds often begin to walk, say a few words, show independence, and exhibit curiosity about their surroundings.

view

When do toddlers usually start walking?

Most toddlers start walking independently between 9 and 15 months.

view

What is amniocentesis?

Amniocentesis is a medical procedure during pregnancy that involves the extraction of a small amount of amniotic fluid from the amniotic sac to analyze and diagnose genetic, chromosomal, and fetal health conditions.

view

Why is amniocentesis performed during pregnancy?

Amniocentesis is performed to assess fetal health, identify genetic and chromosomal disorders, and provide information about the developing baby's well-being.

view

When is amniocentesis typically recommended?

Amniocentesis is usually recommended when there are specific risk factors or indications, such as advanced maternal age, abnormal prenatal screening results, family history of genetic disorders, or known structural abnormalities on ultrasound.

view

  How is amniocentesis performed?

Amniocentesis involves inserting a thin needle through the abdominal wall and into the amniotic sac to collect a sample of amniotic fluid. The procedure is guided by ultrasound to ensure accuracy and safety.

view

    Is amniocentesis painful?

The procedure may cause mild discomfort, but it is typically not described as painful. Local anesthesia is sometimes used to numb the skin before the needle is inserted.

view

What are the risks associated with amniocentesis?

Risks include a small risk of miscarriage (typically less than 1%), infection, leakage of amniotic fluid, and minor discomfort. Your healthcare provider will discuss the risks and benefits with you.

view

How long does the amniocentesis procedure take?

The actual procedure usually takes about 20-30 minutes. However, the entire appointment may take longer, including preparation and recovery time.

view

   What should I expect during and after the amniocentesis?

During amniocentesis, you'll lie on an examination table, and the area will be cleaned. A thin needle is then inserted through the abdomen to collect the fluid. Afterward, you may be monitored briefly, and you'll be given instructions for post-procedure care.

view

What is IVF?

Ans In Vitro Fertilization (IVF) is a fertility treatment where eggs are retrieved from the ovaries and fertilized with sperm outside the body in a lab.

view

How successful is IVF?

Success rates vary based on several factors like age, fertility issues, and clinic expertise. On average, success rates range between 40-50% for women under 35.

view

What does IVF involve?

IVF involves ovarian stimulation, egg retrieval, fertilization, embryo culture, and embryo transfer.

view

Is IVF painful?

The procedure itself isn't painful, but some discomfort might be experienced during egg retrieval and with hormone injections.

view

Who is a candidate for IVF?

Couples facing infertility due to various reasons such as blocked fallopian tubes, male infertility, endometriosis, or unexplained infertility often opt for IVF.

view

How long does an IVF cycle take?

A typical IVF cycle takes around 4-6 weeks from initial testing to embryo transfer.

view

Are there any risks associated with IVF?

Risks include multiple pregnancies, ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS), and a slight increase in the risk of certain birth defects.

view

What are the costs of IVF?

Costs vary widely by location and clinic. On average, a single IVF cycle can range from $12,000 to $15,000.
